# [JCRS] Joint Communication and Radar Perception Multibeam Method Using Manipulable Simulated Array Antennas [with MATLAB Code]

Beamforming has tremendous potential in the field of Joint Communication and Sensory (JCAS), becoming a key requirement for emerging platforms such as unmanned aerial vehicles (UAVs) and intelligent vehicles. Although beamforming has been widely studied in communication and radar perception, its application in joint systems is not straightforward due to the differences in requirements between communication and perception. This paper proposes a novel multi-beam framework based on controllable analog antenna arrays, achieving seamless integration of communication and perception. Unlike traditional JCAS solutions that use a single beam to support joint functions, the core innovation of this framework lies in multi-beam technology: it provides both fixed communication sub-beams and scanning perception sub-beams that vary with data packets through a single transmit array. We designed the system architecture and protocol for this framework to be compatible with modern packet-based communication systems that employ multicarrier modulation. Additionally, we proposed low-complexity and efficient methods for designing and generating multi-beams, which can flexibly meet diverse communication and perception needs. Further development included an algorithm for estimating perception parameters based on traditional digital Fourier transform and one-dimensional compressive sensing techniques, which highly matches the multi-beam framework. Simulation results validate the effectiveness of the proposed framework, beamforming design method, and perception algorithm.
Introduction In emerging platforms such as drones and intelligent vehicles, there is an increasing demand for systems that possess both communication and radar perception capabilities. Radar perception achieves information retrieval and acquisition by estimating the position, velocity, and characteristic signals of targets within the radio signal coverage range. Develop joint communication and radar perception (JCAS) technology, which integrates two functions into a single system through the sharing of hardware and signal processing modules, significantly reduces costs, minimizes size and weight, and enhances spectrum efficiency. The integrated system can also enhance performance through information sharing, such as utilizing sensed environmental information to assist beamforming design.
Millimeter Wave (mmWave) offers an ideal platform for integrated communication and perception functions due to its large bandwidth and small-sized antennas. Large bandwidth not only enables high-speed communication and radar with high resolution but also reduces the information interaction delay between modules. For mmWave, arrays of antennas and controllable beams are needed to overcome large propagation attenuation and estimate signal direction. Beam control can be achieved through fully digital or analog arrays; however, fully digital arrays are costly, while analog arrays are a practical and economical solution. Commercial mmWave phased arrays such as 16×16 60GHz arrays provide JCAS, especially for small platform applications.
Despite extensive research on beamforming in the fields of communication and radar perception, challenges exist in the application of beamforming in JCAS: communication requires stable and highly directional high gain beams, while perception needs time-varying scanning beams. Existing research mostly uses single beams for both communication and perception, resulting in limited perception direction to the direction of the communication node.
This paper explores new multibeam technology, proposing a comprehensive framework for cost-effective and efficient implementation of JCAS on small portable platforms using two analog antenna arrays. As the first attempt to apply multibeam technology in JCAS, this method is significantly different from existing solutions. Multibeam is defined as a beamforming waveform generated by a single analog antenna array that includes two or more main lobes (subbeams): some subbeams have fixed directions, supporting communication and perception in the direction of communication; another part of the subbeams scans different directions within different packet cycles. This framework seamlessly integrates perception with multicarrier modulation and point-to-point communication systems, providing system architecture, working protocols, beamforming design methods, multibeam generation and update algorithms, and perception algorithms to achieve large field of view (direction range), flexible and precise perception, while maintaining controllable and minimal impact on communication performance.

Conclusion This paper proposes a novel multi-beam framework based on controllable simulated antenna arrays, suitable for joint communication and perception in intelligent vehicles, drones, etc. The system architecture, protocols, beamforming schemes, methods for generating and updating multi-beams, as well as perception parameter estimation algorithms were designed. The framework was validated to seamlessly integrate perception into standard TDD packet communication systems employing OFDM modulation, with simulation results confirming the effectiveness of the proposed system, multi-beam generation, and perception algorithm.
This is merely the first step in exploring the potential of multi-beam technology in JCAS. There are still many challenges and areas for improvement in the future, such as generating multi-beam beamforming vectors that quantize amplitude and phase, optimized communication and perception sub-beam combinations according to specific criteria, and perception algorithms that can break through traditional scanning concepts to resolve arrival angle (AoA) and departure angle (AoD) in high-dimensional off-grid models.
